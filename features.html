Features

Everything it does.

A broadcast-style score bug for your desktop — what it shows, and how it behaves on your screen.

On screen

Built like a broadcast

Scoring alerts that tell you something

A flashing run-up covers the bar, then the score lands with who scored, the actual play, and what it was worth in fantasy.

J. Gibbs 29 CAR, 156 YDS, 2 TD · 5 REC, 30 YDS 33.6 FPTS

Fantasy points on every line

Scored the way each sport is really scored — ESPN PPR for football, DraftKings for basketball and hockey, Fantasy Premier League for soccer — off the full box score, so a back's receiving work counts.

A bar or a box

Drag an edge. Narrow it past half your screen and the ticker stacks itself into a corner box, logos and all. Drag it back and it spreads out again.

Or run it in a tab

Same bug, nothing to install. On a Chromebook or a laptop, press Pop out and it lifts into its own small window that stays on top of everything else — on ChromeOS that is the only way to get an overlay at all, since apps in the Linux container can't float above ChromeOS windows.

Keep it square. The pop-out is a few hundred pixels wide, which is exactly where the ticker folds into its box — that is the shape to leave it. Stretched wide it has to cram a full broadcast bar into a window with no room for one, and everything shrinks to fit.

Computers and laptops only. Phones and tablets cannot float a window over what you are doing — the operating system simply does not allow it — so there is no pop-out to press. The page still loads and fills the screen, but a score bug that cannot sit on top of anything is not really the point.

Upsets, in every league

Rank where there is one, division where there isn't — an FCS side beating an FBS side counts — and record everywhere else, with a mismatch threshold tuned per sport because baseball records bunch up.

Oklahoma St upset #6 Oregon 39–31

Your fantasy league, live

Paste a Sleeper or public ESPN league link. Matchups as they move, the week's top performers across the whole league, standings, and your league's own scoring settings — in a sidebar that pins, scrolls itself and collapses.

It stays out of the way

Clicks pass straight through the empty space around it, and it never takes focus — so a fullscreen game stays fullscreen instead of dropping you to the desktop.
